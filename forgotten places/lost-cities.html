Machu Picchu

Machu Picchu is an ancient Inca city located high in the Andes Mountains of Peru. Built during the 15th century, it stands on a mountain ridge surrounded by steep green peaks and deep valleys. The city contains stone houses, terraces, temples and pathways carefully constructed without modern machinery. Its exact purpose is still discussed by historians, although it is believed to have served as a royal estate or ceremonial center. After the Spanish conquest of the Inca Empire, Machu Picchu was gradually abandoned and became largely unknown outside the local region for centuries. In 1911, it gained international attention through the expedition of Hiram Bingham. Today, its impressive architecture and dramatic location make it one of the most famous ancient sites in the world.

Location: Cusco Region, Peru

Pompeii

Pompeii was a thriving Roman city near Mount Vesuvius in southern Italy. It had houses, shops, public baths, temples, streets and a large amphitheater. In AD 79, Mount Vesuvius erupted, covering the city in volcanic ash and other materials. The eruption buried Pompeii and preserved many details of everyday Roman life, including buildings, decorations and objects left behind by its inhabitants. The city remained buried for many centuries before systematic excavations revealed its remarkable remains. Archaeologists have learned a great deal about Roman society from its streets, homes and public spaces. Although Pompeii was destroyed, it has become an extraordinary historical site that allows visitors to imagine what life was like in an ancient Roman city.

Location: Campania, Italy

Petra

Petra is an ancient city in Jordan, famous for its monumental buildings carved directly into rose-colored sandstone cliffs. It became an important center of the Nabataean civilization, partly because of its position along historic trading routes. Merchants transported goods such as spices and incense through the region, bringing wealth to the city. Petra's inhabitants developed sophisticated water collection and storage systems that helped them live in the dry environment. Over time, changes in trade routes and other historical developments reduced the city's importance, and many of its structures were gradually forgotten by the outside world. Petra later attracted international attention and is now recognized as one of the world's most remarkable archaeological sites. Its monumental facades and narrow rock passages reveal the creativity and skill of its builders.

Location: Ma'an Governorate, Jordan

Angkor

Angkor was the center of the Khmer Empire in present-day Cambodia. Between the 9th and 15th centuries, it developed into an enormous urban area containing temples, reservoirs, canals and roads. Its most famous monument, Angkor Wat, was originally built as a Hindu temple and later became an important Buddhist religious site. The city's water management systems helped support a large population and extensive agricultural activity. Over time, political changes, environmental pressures and other factors contributed to the decline of Angkor as an imperial center. Many temples and structures remained in the landscape, while vegetation gradually covered parts of the ruins. Today, Angkor offers a fascinating look at the achievements of the Khmer civilization and attracts visitors interested in history, architecture and archaeology.

Location: Siem Reap Province, Cambodia

Why Were These Cities Abandoned?

  • Natural disasters and volcanic eruptions
  • Changes in trade routes and economic activity
  • Political conflicts and the decline of empires
  • Environmental changes and difficulties obtaining resources
