Oculus Tower

At first glance, the Oculus Tower looks more like a futuristic arena than part of an old distillery. Rising from the remains of the former Eridania industrial complex in Ferrara, this seven-story circular concrete structure was once the heart of a large alcohol-production plant.
The tower's most striking feature is its enormous open center. The seven levels form a series of galleries around the void, which once housed distillation equipment. Above it, a large glass skylight—or oculus—flooded the interior with natural light. Rows of windows punctuate the reinforced-concrete frame, giving the building an unexpected resemblance to a modern Colosseum.
The distillery processed beet molasses into raw spirits, refined spirits, and denatured alcohol used in products ranging from liqueurs to cosmetics and pharmaceuticals. After the industrial complex closed in the 2000s, much of the site was gradually demolished, leaving the circular tower as its most recognizable survivor.
Today, the Oculus Tower stands as an unusual piece of Italian industrial archaeology: monumental, geometric, and strangely theatrical, even without the machinery that once filled its vast interior.

Location: 18 Via Luigi Turchi Ferrara, 44122 Italy

Abandoned Agricultural Mill

Tucked away in the Polish countryside stands an impressive early 20th-century agricultural mill complex that looks more like a grand estate than a heavy industrial facility. Dominated by a striking multi-story central tower with a decorative roof, the building represents a era when industrial architecture was built with notable aesthetic ambition.
Inside, the structure retains an eerie atmosphere of suspended time. A grand wooden and iron spiral staircase coils through the main tower, leading to spacious upper levels where vintage machinery, grain chutes, and wooden sifting equipment remain in place. Sunlight filters through arched windows and ivy-covered sashes, illuminating rooms that still hold original wooden doors, vintage lace curtains, and scattered remnants of daily operations. Though decay has set in, the complex remains an extraordinary capsule of Poland's rural industrial heritage.

Villa de Vecchi

Hidden among the forested mountains near Lake Como in northern Italy, Villa de Vecchi is a mysterious abandoned mansion known as the "Ghost Mansion" or "House of Witches." Built between 1854 and 1857 as a summer residence for Count Felix de Vecchi, the villa once featured elegant gardens, decorated walls, grand rooms, and a magnificent fireplace.

After the family left the property following World War II, the mansion eventually fell into ruin. Nature slowly reclaimed its surroundings, while graffiti and years of neglect damaged its once-beautiful interiors. Despite its ruined appearance, the building still carries an atmosphere of faded luxury and mystery.

Location: Cortenova, near Lake Como, Italy

Abandoned Dunnington Mansion

Located in Farmville, Virginia, the Dunnington Mansion is a historic Victorian residence with a fascinating past. Its history dates back to 1748, but the house was transformed into an impressive mansion during the 1890s. Its distinctive entrance and Victorian tower once gave the property an elegant appearance.

The mansion stopped being a cared-for residence in 2000 after plans to turn it into a golf club building failed. Left empty as the surrounding golf course was developed, it gradually deteriorated. Preservation efforts later began to document its history and protect what remains of this remarkable building.

Location: Farmville, Virginia, USA

The Abandoned Monorail of Melaka

In the historic Malaysian city of Melaka, an abandoned monorail stands as a reminder of an ambitious transportation project that never became successful. Built in 2008 and completed in 2010, the elevated railway was designed to serve The Shore, a large residential and commercial development along the Melaka River.

However, the monorail faced serious technical problems from the very beginning. On its opening day, a train stopped unexpectedly, leaving 20 passengers trapped inside. After repeated breakdowns and several closures, the service was suspended in 2013. It briefly returned in 2017, but the COVID-19 pandemic brought its operations to another halt in 2020. With very few passengers using the system, it eventually became permanently abandoned.

Today, much of the infrastructure remains in place. Elevated tracks form a strange loop above the city, while abandoned carriages and remnants of the old facilities recall a project that promised modern transportation but failed to attract enough passengers. What was once meant to represent progress has become an unusual urban relic, frozen between ambition and decay.

Location: Jalan Tun Ali, Melaka, Malaysia
